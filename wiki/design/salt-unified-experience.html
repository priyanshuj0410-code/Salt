Salt — one experience, seven views

Reconciling the 7 persona IAs into a single adaptive app — where a “persona” is a derived view, and duties can be shared or absent.

There were never seven apps — there is one experience, projected differently for whoever's looking. A view is a function of three knobs: which capability atoms you hold, your altitude on each (watch · operate · file), and your context (counter · desk · road · offline). The seven personas are just the seven common presets; the real world freely mixes them (the owner who also dispenses and bills), drops them (no field salesman), or shares them (a cashier and a pharmacist on one counter). Below: toggle capabilities and watch the single app recompose. Load a persona preset and it re-derives exactly the IA built for that role — one projector, seven projections.

The model — one substrate, a lens per user

01One substrate

Every capability and all the lifecycle data live in one app — the union of all seven IAs. Nothing is role-specific at the data layer; the counter, the books, the beat all read and write the same tables.

02A lens, not an app

What a user sees is a projection: the union of their capability atoms, arranged by altitude and context. Change the atoms and the Home, primary surface, persistent action, lit nav and inline gates all recompose — live.

03Personas are presets

Each of the seven is a named bundle of atoms + a default altitude. They're starting points, not cages — merge them, drop them, or hand-assign atoms to a real person. The persona is emergent, not built.

Shared, absent, solo — how the real world bends it

Shared duties

A capability attaches to whoever holds it — and more than one person can. Events route to any holder present: an approval to any approver, an H1 gate to any pharmacist on shift, a collection to whoever's on the beat. The interlocks bind to a capability, never to “the manager” by name.

Absent duties

An atom no one holds doesn't vanish — the stage and its data still exist. The system flags it as a gap (“no one is assigned to reconcile GST — books may drift”), or it folds up to the owner by default. There is no “missing app”, only an unassigned job.

The solo operator

One person holding every hat gets the union — but it stays calm through altitude + progressive disclosure + mode-switch: the counter is front-and-centre by day (80% of his time), books & GST tucked but present by evening, gates inline. Still one app, one lens — just a wide one.

What dissolves — the per-persona elements become derived, not hand-built
Real-shop validation — a Varanasi wholesale pharmacy

A live stress test: a wholesale pharmacy with 2 owners who both man counters, a third counter run by an employee, 3–4 in-shop salespeople across the floor and godown, 4 field salesmen, and an external CA for tax & compliance. What held up

No manager → atoms redistribute to owners / godown / field; no empty screen. Operating owners → altitude flips the landing to the counter. The employee's over-limit routes to any owner (a capability, not a name). 2 owners · 4 field · ~3 shop = multi-instance + 3-counter realtime. The field offline/scan substrate is reused by the godown.

What it surfaced

✦ additive — a pick / fulfil atom (godown picking) and a wholesale supply-compliance atom (buyer licence + supply register; C was retail-shaped). ⚠ off-axis — the external CA's access model (scoped, expiring, multi-shop, read + export) is tenancy, not IA. And the model correctly flags a gap: with only a tax CA, daily books may drift.

The V3 verdict

Every org-shape divergence was config + derivation — no restructure. The two domain gaps are additive atoms (13 → 15, now live in the composer). The one off-axis item (external access) is exactly the conscious bet named earlier — the test found it, which is the model working. No V3.
