Salt — User Personas

7 people in the loop · tier-2 / tier-3 India · English is not their first language · Marg keyboard power-users (the field salesman is the exception)

Hindi / regional mother tongue Marg muscle memory Internet drops 2–5×/day WhatsApp + phone first

Seven people keep the shop running — the five app roles plus two kinds of salespeople: the shop salesman at the counter and the field salesman who books orders and delivers on a beat. They share a Marg habit and a language reality, but behave very differently when the screen changes, the internet drops, or they get stuck — and the field salesman isn't on Marg or a counter at all. This tab is the shared ground and a cross-persona comparison matrix so no behaviour gets missed; the other tabs go deep on each.

The ground they all stand on

🏪 The setting

A 600–1,500 sq ft shop in a district-HQ town (3–15 lakh people), often in the dawa bazaar near the hospital. 2–5 billing counters, floor-to-ceiling racks, a godown behind. Loud, fast, physical — 5–15 customers present at peak.

⌨️ Marg is the muscle

Everyone learned the trade on Marg ERP 9+. The real skill that transfers between staff is the keystroke sequence, not the screen. A new hire is "trained on Marg" in 2–3 days. Any change to that sequence costs days of re-learning.

📶 Unreliable everything

Jio/Airtel broadband drops 2–5× a day, worse in monsoon; phone-hotspot is the backup. The phone is the trusted device; WhatsApp is how orders, bills, statements and reminders actually move.

Language & literacy — how "English is not first language" shows up

🔤 How they read & type

  • They type in roman-script Hinglish — paracetamol, ramesh medical, baaki kitna hai — never Devanagari at the counter.
  • They scan numbers and positions, not sentences. A screen of English prose is wallpaper; the eye hunts for the digit.
  • They read drug names fluently (learned as shapes/tokens) but UI English is opaque — "reconcile", "allocate", "void", "post" don't land.
  • Numbers must be Indian format — ₹1,00,000, lakh / crore. Western grouping 100,000 reads as foreign and wrong.

🧭 What this means at the screen

  • An English error dialog is a dead end — they don't read it; they react to "something is wrong" and call someone senior.
  • They learn by demonstration (aise karo) and rote, not by reading help text or tooltips.
  • Icons + position + colour-as-state carry the meaning; the English label is secondary.
  • Trust is built by matching the screen to something physical — cash in hand, the strip, the bank SMS, their own register.
The apps they all already know — patterns Salt inherits for free
  • WhatsApp — chat, voice notes, forward a PDF, Status broadcast, blue-tick read receipts. Sharing = "send on WhatsApp" (never email); a tick = confirmation; voice note > typing.
  • UPI — Google Pay / PhonePe / Paytm — scan-to-pay, instant green check + sound, recent payees, pay-by-contact. A money action is one tap with instant, audible + visual confirmation. This is the bar for "saved".
  • Paytm / PhonePe soundbox — calls out "payment received" aloud at the counter. Important events should be heard, not just seen — hands and eyes are busy.
  • YouTube — they learn anything by watching a Hindi video; search the problem, watch, copy. Help is a short video, not a manual or a tooltip.
  • Truecaller — a name on an unknown number. Recognition beats recall; trust comes from identity on a contact, not a code.
